TL;DR

Problem
The Maintenance Trap
Admins were drowning in maintenance work. The high total cost of ownership had quietly become a churn driver worth hundreds of millions — and the org had been deadlocked on what to do about it for years.
Solution
Show, Don't Tell
I built a live prototype that proved what the admin experience could be, then walked it into a C-suite room. The deadlock broke in 45 minutes. We didn't need more features — we needed to get out of the admin's way.
Outcome
6 Teams, One SGO
The session secured six engineering teams and a company-level Strategic Growth Objective. The Glass Box framework born here became Workday's AI governance standard.

Key Features

The 45-Minute Prototype

A working prototype beats a year of decks. I showed the executives the future instead of describing it — and the multi-year debate resolved in under an hour.

Live prototype shown in the C-suite session

Getting Out of the Way

The redesign automated the boring maintenance and surfaced only what an admin actually needs to decide. Less interface, more outcome.

Before/after: cluttered maintenance vs. focused admin view

The Glass Box

Trust at the moment of action — the system shows its reasoning and lets the admin intervene. This pattern outgrew the project and became the company standard.

Glass Box pattern in the admin context

Deep Dive

Breaking the DeadlockWhat actually moved the room

Why decks failed

Years of strategy slides hadn't moved it. A tangible prototype did, because it removed the ambiguity everyone was arguing inside of.

The business framing

I led with the churn number, not the design. Revenue language opened the door that craft language couldn't.

ReflectionSpeed, visibility, and credit

What worked

Pairing a revenue argument with a working artifact. Either alone would have stalled.

What I'd refine

Bringing partners into the messy middle earlier — the speed read as magic, and magic is hard to build on.
