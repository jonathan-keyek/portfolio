Jonathan Keyek

Principal Product Designer

I'm a product designer. My craft is the actual experience — the patterns, the components, the moment-to-moment interactions people touch — and I've spent my career making complex software feel simple, beautiful, and humane.

What's unusual is where I came from: avant-garde filmmaking and computer science. Filmmaking taught me to describe a feeling in vivid detail until it's undeniable; computer science taught me to build the thing myself when that's what it takes. But the design always comes first. The tech is just how I get my craft into people's hands faster and more faithfully.

For the past eight years at Workday, I've designed HR and financial experiences that ship — and change how millions of people actually work. I design the conversational and AI patterns that frontline employees, HR administrators, and developers use every day, including Workday's ambient agents: AI that works quietly alongside people inside the flow of their jobs rather than demanding their attention. The work I do becomes the product, not a picture of it.

That means designing the system, not just the screen: the interaction patterns that align dozens of teams behind one coherent experience, the standards that make AI trustworthy, the internal tooling that lifts an entire design org. Along the way the craft has earned its keep — tens of millions in contracts signed, millions more saved by killing ill-conceived features before they shipped, and real gains in how people experience their workday.

I'm still happiest at the bezier curve and the command line. That's where the craft lives — and where the vision gets built, one pattern, one component, one interaction at a time, until the details add up to a complete and comprehensive picture of what could be.
