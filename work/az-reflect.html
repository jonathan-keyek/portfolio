Pilot Retention Over 30 Days
Every participant returned to track health data and send reflections — without a single reminder.

TL;DR

Problem
The Memory Gap
The clinical data AZ needed lived inside patients who were sick, stressed, and medicated — the exact conditions that destroy self-reporting. Traditional trackers felt like homework, so patients stopped. You can't collect data from someone who's checked out.
Solution
The Lasagna Strategy
I hid the vegetables inside the lasagna. Instead of a health tracker, I built a family connection app where telling your loved ones how you're doing happened to capture the clinical data doctors needed. Connection was the hook. Data was the passenger.
Outcome
System Genesis
Every pilot participant returned for 30 days, unprompted — proving emotional engagement drives clinical compliance. As a bonus, I reverse-engineered the UI into AstraZeneca's first official design system.

Key Features

The Camera That Reads You

She's not filling out a form — she's recording a video for her family. The app reads vitals through the camera (40 bpm, 98.8°F, emotional state) before she says a word. By the time she hits Start Reflection, the data is already captured.

Camera UI: vitals resolving during a selfie scan, no form field in sight

Vital Tracking, Hidden in Plain Sight

What the camera captures becomes a health record — surfaced quietly under the reflection, not announced as a clinical event. Patients weren't logging vitals; they were finishing a story.

Reflection detail view with auto-generated breathing rate, emotion, and health notes

The Engagement Loop

Patients didn't return because we asked. They returned because their family responded — comments, likes, new members joining their Reflection Family. That social loop, not any reminder, drove 30-day retention.

Social feed: family reactions and comment threads on a reflection

The Living Room Aesthetic

Blue meant hospital. Red meant danger. We landed on Teal/Cream — warm enough to feel consumer, calm enough to feel safe. The app needed to look like a message from a friend, not an appointment reminder.

Marketing site + mobile, Teal-on-Cream palette

Deep Dive

Validation & TestingWhat 30 days of real patient data looked like

How we tested

Five participants, thirty days, unmoderated in-home use — no lab, no facilitator. The goal: see whether the Lasagna Strategy held up with no researcher watching.

What happened

Every participant came back. All five. Across 30 days. The engagement didn't drop off after week one the way every prior tool had.

The honest caveat

n=5 is a pilot, not a proof. There was no pre-existing baseline — the internal belief was that consistent data collection from this cohort wasn't achievable at all. What the pilot proved was that the hypothesis was worth pursuing at scale.

Reflection & LearningDesigning for people who have no energy left

What worked

The reframe was the strategy, not spin. The emotional hook was load-bearing — every engagement metric traced back to making family connection the primary value.

What I'd do differently

More participants, faster. Test the language layer earlier and wider — the words were as important as the interactions, and we treated them as a downstream concern when they were upstream.

The lesson

Compliance is a design problem, not a willpower problem. Design for the actual human in front of you, not the compliant patient you wish you had.

Impact

"This changes communication for me. This changes my relationship with my illness and my family, massively, for the better."

— Doug, Pilot Participant
