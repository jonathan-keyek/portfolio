Redesigning the Ford F-150 cockpit

Client
Ford (Personal Concept)
Year
2024
Role
Solo — Research, Strategy, Motion & Visual
200ms
The Scan Window
The industry threshold for safe instrumentation legibility at speed — the constraint every decision was built around.

TL;DR

Problem
Data Asphyxiation
I bought a new F-150 and the dashboard wasn't giving me information — it was throwing it at me. Status, warnings, navigation, infotainment, all competing for the same two eyeballs that should be on the road. Dashboards are designed around what the car knows, not what the driver needs next.
Solution
Blackout Mode
Strip the cluster to only what matters at this moment, at this speed. Everything else recedes. The 200ms scan window became the hard constraint: if a driver can't read it in a glance, it doesn't belong on the screen.
Outcome
A Calmer Cockpit
A cluster that reads in 200ms, a Handshake Protocol for handing control between driver and system, and a motion language validated by a Google UX Director.

Key Features

Blackout Mode

The default state shows almost nothing — speed, and whatever the moment demands. Information earns its way onto the screen instead of starting there.

Blackout Mode cluster: minimal at speed

The Handshake Protocol

When the car wants to take or return control, the transition is an explicit, legible handshake — not a silent mode change. Trust comes from knowing who's driving.

Handshake Protocol: control transition states

200ms Legibility

Every element was tested against the glance budget. Typography, contrast, and motion all serve one goal: comprehension in a fifth of a second.

Legibility study: glance-timed cluster reads
